# Sources — Masjid al-Haram 3D Explorer (`/haram`)

The place descriptions in the explorer (`haram-src/src/data/places.ts`) are written from the
sources below. Each description lists its sources in the information panel, so readers can
check every statement themselves. Descriptions paraphrase these sources; they do not quote
translations at length.

When editing a description, keep to what these sources support. Do not add dates,
measurements, traditions or claims of significance that they do not state. Anything that is
approximate in the **3D model** belongs in the place's `modelNote`, not in its description.

Source ids match `haram-src/src/data/sources.ts`.

## Deliberate omissions

Where sources disagreed or could not be confirmed, the explorer leaves the detail out rather
than guess:

- **King Fahd Gate** is not modelled: sources consulted disagree about which side of the
  mosque it is on.
- **Gate numbers** are given only where consistent (Gate 1, Gate 100). Bab al-Umrah's number
  differs between sources and is omitted.
- No lengths are stated for the Mas'a or the green-lit section, and no dates for the Mataf
  expansions.
- No **Multazam** entry: it was left out rather than describe its associated practices without
  a source that was reviewed for this project. It can be added once one is.

## Modelling references (approximate)

The 3D model is an approximate representation (see `HARAM.md`, "What is approximate"). The
few dimensions it uses come from:

- Kaaba: about 13.1 m high, sides about 11.03 m × 12.86 m, corners roughly towards the compass
  points, Black Stone in the eastern corner about 1.5 m up, door on the north-eastern wall about
  2 m up, marble base about 25 cm high projecting about 35 cm (Wikipedia — Kaaba; Wikipedia —
  Black Stone).
- Kiswah layout: belt about two-thirds of the way up, about 95 cm wide, four pieces per side;
  a square medallion panel below the belt at each corner; door curtain about 7.75 m × 3.5 m
  (Wikipedia — Kiswah). Panel sizes and the curtain's exact extent are approximate.
- Zamzam about 21 m east of the Kaaba, towards Maqam Ibrahim, in line with the Multazam
  (Saudipedia; IslamicLandmarks.com; Wikipedia — Zamzam Well). The exact spot is approximate.
- Gate sides (Madain Project); clock tower height (CTBUH); clock faces 43 m across, with the
  hands, colours, emblem and inscriptions described in Wikipedia — Abraj Al Bait. The Saudi
  coat of arms is drawn from the Basic Law's description (two crossed swords, hilts at the
  base, a palm tree above and between the blades).
- The Mataf's marble in rows facing the Kaaba: the visible layout of the real floor. Tile size
  (1.6 m) and the corner fans are approximate.
- The Black Stone's frame: pure silver, oval, about 30 cm across, renewed in 1979 and 2001; eight
  fragments visible in a brownish paste (Saudipedia — The Black Stone); its shape — a broad
  polished plate with a pointed foot, wrapped round the corner, with a rolled rim round the
  opening — from photographs. The plate's size is estimated.
- Maqam Ibrahim: the stone about 50 cm square in Saudi sources (40 × 40 × 20 cm in Wikipedia),
  its two footprints about 27 × 14 cm and 9–10 cm deep; a crystal cover 1.30 m high and 80 cm
  across at its foot; a cage of gold-plated brass with a gilded inner grille; a base of Carrara
  marble trimmed with green granite (Saudipedia — Maqam Ibrahim; Arabic Wikipedia — مقام
  إبراهيم). The cage's eight-sided shape, roof, dome and crescent from photographs; its size
  and the base's height are estimated from them.
- Inside the Kaaba: the floor about 2 m above the Mataf (the door's sill); the room's finishes,
  pillars, lamps, cupboard, plaques and Bab al-Tawbah from the photographs in IslamicLandmarks.com
  — Inside the Ka'bah and descriptions in Wikipedia — Kaaba. The room's measurements, the
  ceiling's height, the pillars' spacing and the number of lamps are not published and are
  approximate.

- The plan — the courtyard, the Ottoman portico, the halls, the Mas'a, the expansions, the
  gates, the minarets, Maqam Ibrahim's position, the Kaaba's orientation (door wall facing about
  56.5° from north), the clock tower's position and the buildings around — from OpenStreetMap
  (`osm-haram`), simplified to within about a metre.

Everything else — Hijr Ismail's exact curve, the exact sizes of the Maqam's enclosure and the
Black Stone's frame, the room inside the Kaaba, heights and interiors of the halls, the generic
city and the mountains — is approximate or schematic and was not taken from a survey.
